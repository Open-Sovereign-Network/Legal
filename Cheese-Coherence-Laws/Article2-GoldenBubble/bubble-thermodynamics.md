# Bubble Thermodynamics

## Article 2, Section 1: The Physics of Crust Bubbles

### 1.1 Bubble Formation Principles

Bubbles within the pizza crust represent localized regions of gas expansion governed by the ideal gas law and modified by gluten network constraints.

### 1.2 Temperature Thresholds

Optimal bubble formation occurs when:
- Oven temperature: 450-500°C (850-950°F)
- Internal dough temperature reaches: 95-100°C
- Steam pressure exceeds gluten tensile strength: >2 kPa

### 1.3 Golden Ratio

The surface area of bubbles shall ideally occupy 15-25% of the total crust surface to achieve the perfect balance between structural integrity and aesthetic appeal.

### 1.4 Heat Transfer Dynamics

The rate of heat transfer through the dough matrix determines bubble size distribution according to:

```
dQ/dt = k·A·(ΔT/Δx)
```

Where:
- k = thermal conductivity of dough
- A = surface area
- ΔT = temperature differential
- Δx = thickness

### 1.5 Leopard Spotting

The characteristic charred spots (leoparding) shall result from direct contact with oven surfaces exceeding 480°C, creating localized Maillard reactions and caramelization.

## Thermodynamic Violations

Insufficient bubble formation or over-densification constitutes a failure to achieve proper crust architecture.
