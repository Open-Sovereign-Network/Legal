# Crust-Crumb Continuum

## Article 2, Section 2: The Structural Integrity Framework

### 2.1 Crumb Structure Definition

The crumb shall be defined as the internal structure of the dough, characterized by:
- Alveoli (air pockets) distribution
- Gluten network organization
- Moisture gradient from exterior to interior

### 2.2 Continuum Principle

The crust and crumb exist on a continuum of doneness, moisture, and structural density, with no discrete boundary but rather a gradual transition zone.

### 2.3 Hydration Levels

Optimal dough hydration shall be maintained at:
- Neapolitan style: 55-60%
- New York style: 60-65%
- Roman style: 70-80%

### 2.4 Fermentation Time

The development of proper crumb structure requires minimum fermentation periods:
- Room temperature: 8-24 hours
- Cold fermentation: 24-72 hours

### 2.5 Crisp-to-Chew Ratio

The exterior crust shall provide initial resistance (crisp) followed by a yielding interior structure (chew), with the transition occurring within 2-3mm of the surface.

### 2.6 Structural Equations

The Young's modulus of the crust shall exceed that of the crumb by a factor of 3-5 to maintain proper textural contrast.

## Continuum Violations

Deviation from the prescribed crust-crumb gradient indicates improper baking conditions or dough preparation failures.
